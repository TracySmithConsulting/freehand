# Three-Sound — script draft 1

**Format:** audio-only. Recorded voice, two soft chimes, three silences.
**Length target:** ~10 minutes total.
**Voice:** Tracy's first person, addressed to the listener ("you"), plain words, no affirmations.
**Files planned:** script as markdown, final recording as a single 10-minute `.wav`.

---

## Pre-listening  (~2 min, ~250 words, one chime at the end)

You're about to do an exercise. It lasts about ten minutes, and it has
three parts.

You don't need to do anything. Sit, or stand, or lie down. Wherever you
are is fine. If something needs your attention — a child, an alarm, a
phone — handle it, then come back when it's quieter. There's no rush.

For the next two minutes, you're going to listen to whatever is already
in the room. Don't add anything. Don't try to make it quiet. Just notice
the sounds that are there. The ones you've stopped hearing. The ones
you usually talk over. The low hum that might be the building, the
small clicks that might be a fan, whatever your hands are resting on.

If you notice your mind starting to think about something, that's fine.
Notice that too. Then go back to listening.

[a single soft chime — then a 30-second silence]

---

## Three sounds  (~6 min, four chimes, three silences between prompts)

Listen for one sound near you. Close enough to touch.

Hold it for one breath.

Notice how your ears reach toward it. Notice what gives it away —
sharpness, or steadiness, or the way it changes.

Let it go.

[a chime — 20 seconds of silence]

Now listen for one sound in the middle distance. Across the room, or
just outside the door, or downstairs.

Hold it for two breaths.

Notice how it sits between the near sound and the far sound. Notice
whether it changes when you breathe.

Let it go.

[a chime — 20 seconds of silence]

Now listen for one sound in the far distance. Outside, or far away, or
just the depth of the room.

Hold it for three breaths.

Notice how your ears go out to meet it. Notice whether your body
changes when you listen that far.

Let it go.

[a longer chime — a 60-second silence]

---

## Return  (~2 min, ~200 words, one final chime)

You're back.

You might notice the room has changed slightly. It hasn't — but your
listening has. What was background a moment ago is foreground now.
That shift is what you take with you.

For the next minute, just be where you are.

If you want to remember what you heard, you can. If you don't, that's
fine too.

When you're ready, open your eyes, or stand up, or turn back to
whatever you were doing. The exercise is over.

[a final soft chime — silence]

---

## Notes for the recording session

**Voice:** keep it conversational, not guided-meditation. Words sit on
a breath, not on a chant. No "calm" or "release" or "let go" in a
spiritual sense — only their literal meaning. No music.

**Chime:** one tone, soft, around 440 Hz, fading out over about 1
second. The same chime three times in pre-listening, four times in
the middle phase (after each prompt and after the last breath), once
at the end. Different chime for the very end is fine — a fifth,
slightly lower pitch, to mark the close.

**Silence length is part of the script** — the chimes are there to
mark the boundaries. Don't truncate a silence to "move things along";
it is the practice.

**Recording environment:** any quiet room. The script doesn't need
studio silence — the small sounds are part of what you might hear.
Try not to record next to a fan pointed straight at the mic, but a
warm room is fine.

**Take it slow.** The target length is ten minutes; if you hit twelve,
that's better than nine. Breaths at the pace of speech, not at the
pace of reading.

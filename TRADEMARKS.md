# The FreeHand name

The FreeHand **code** is free to use under the [MIT licence](LICENSE).
The FreeHand **name** and logo are not covered by that licence. They belong to
Tracy Smith Consulting, so that people always know which FreeHand is the
original and who stands behind it.

In short: **take the code, and give your version its own name.**

## You can, without asking

- Say that your project is "based on FreeHand", "a fork of FreeHand" or
  "works with FreeHand".
- Use the name to talk about FreeHand: in reviews, articles, videos,
  tutorials, talks and lessons.
- Keep the name in your own copy while you work on it, for example a fork on
  GitHub that you use to send fixes back to this project.
- Run FreeHand unchanged for yourself, your family or your organisation.

## Please ask first

- Before you use the name or logo in a product name, a company name, a website
  address or an app-store listing.
- Before you sell merchandise with the name or logo on it.

Ask by opening an issue on this project. Most reasonable requests get a yes.

## Please don't

- Publish, sell or host a changed version and call it "FreeHand".
- Make your project look like the official FreeHand, or suggest that we
  support or approve it when we don't.
- Use a name or logo so close to FreeHand's that people could mix them up.

## Why this matters

When someone downloads "FreeHand", they are trusting that it is the version
built to protect their privacy, their accounts and their memories. A copy
under the same name could change any of that without them knowing. A new name
lets everyone tell the versions apart, and that protects your users as well as
ours.

For a plain-language guide to what the licence allows, see
[Using, sharing and changing FreeHand](docs/LICENSING.md).

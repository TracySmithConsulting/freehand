# Using, sharing and changing FreeHand

This page explains, in everyday language, what you are allowed to do with
FreeHand. The legal wording is in the [LICENSE](../LICENSE) file. If this page
and that file ever disagree, the LICENSE file is the one that counts. This
page is a guide, not legal advice.

## The short version

FreeHand is **open source**. That means:

- **Using it is free.** Use it at home, at work, or for a business. You don't have to pay or ask permission.
- **You can see how it works.** All the code is public, so anyone can check what FreeHand does with your data.
- **You can change it.** Change anything you like, for yourself or for others.
- **You can share it.** Give it away or sell it, changed or unchanged.

There are only two things to remember:

1. **Keep the copyright notice.** Any copy you share must still include the
   LICENSE file. It says who made FreeHand.
2. **Use a different name for your own version.** The code is free to take,
   but the FreeHand name is not. See [The FreeHand name](../TRADEMARKS.md).

## Words you may come across

**Licence:** The permission slip that comes with software. It says what you may and may not do with it. FreeHand uses the MIT licence, one of the most widely used and most generous ones.

**Open source:** Software whose code is public, under a licence that lets anyone use, change and share it.

**Repository (or "repo"):** The online folder that holds all of FreeHand's code and its history.

**Fork:** Your own copy of a project's code, which you can change freely. Forking is a normal, welcome part of open source. For example, people fork a project to fix something for themselves, or to try an idea before suggesting it.

**Pull request:** A suggestion to add your changes to the original project. The project's owner decides whether to accept it.

**Trademark:** A name or logo that tells people who a product comes from. It is protected separately from the code. That's why a fork that becomes its own product gets a new name. For example, LibreOffice began as a fork of OpenOffice and took a new name because the OpenOffice name belonged to someone else.

## Common questions

**Can I use FreeHand for my business?**
Yes, at no cost.

**Can I set up FreeHand for other people and charge them for my time?**
Yes. Charging for installation, hosting, support or training is fine. If you
describe your service, say that it "runs FreeHand". Don't present yourself as
the maker of FreeHand.

**Can I change FreeHand and share my version?**
Yes. Keep the LICENSE file in it, and give your version its own name.

**Can I send my improvements back to FreeHand?**
Yes, please do. Open a pull request. By sending one, you agree that your
changes can be shared under the same MIT licence as the rest of FreeHand.

**Does FreeHand come with a guarantee?**
No. Like most free software, FreeHand is provided "as is". The people who make
it do their best, but they can't promise it will never go wrong, and they
aren't responsible if it does. Keep backups of anything important.

**What about the other software FreeHand uses?**
FreeHand is built with other open-source projects, such as OpenConnector,
Hindsight and faster-whisper. Each one keeps its own licence, which you'll
find with that project.

## Questions?

Open an issue on the FreeHand repository and ask. There are no silly
questions.
